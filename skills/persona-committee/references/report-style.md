# Report Style

The report is read by busy marketers, product managers and executives. Write it the way a top journalist writes a front-page explainer: clear, specific, easy to follow, and worth reading.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence of every section says what happened. Detail comes after.
2. **Short sentences.** Aim for 15 words. Break any sentence over 25 words.
3. **One idea per paragraph.** Two to three sentences.
4. **Show, don't summarize.** Never write an abstract pattern on its own. Pair it with a literal example.
   - Weak: "CoreWeave stacks third-party proof in a fixed order."
   - Strong: "CoreWeave's homepage opens with an outside judge, not a claim: *'CoreWeave earns SemiAnalysis' highest ClusterMAX™ rating for the third consecutive time.'* The reader meets the referee's verdict before the vendor says a word about itself."
5. **Quote exactly.** Competitor and buyer quotes are verbatim, in quotation marks, with the source and date. Never paraphrase inside quotation marks.
6. **Name people and numbers.** "Clara, the CFO" beats "the finance persona". "3 of 9" beats "some".
7. **Plain words.** Say "fits their job", not "JTBD alignment". Say "believable", not "credibility vector". Explain any unavoidable term in five words or fewer.
8. **No hedging stacks.** One qualifier per claim at most.
9. **No em-dashes.** Use commas, periods or parentheses.
10. **Honest about limits.** Say plainly what the committee cannot tell you. Never overstate synthetic feedback as buyer truth.

## Voice of the personas

Persona lines in the dialog are in each persona's own voice: first person, their vocabulary, their skepticism. They are labeled synthetic in the report.

## Banned phrases in report prose

leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, holistic, synergy, best-in-class, world-class, game-changer, cutting-edge, delve, landscape (as a metaphor), "in today's fast-paced", "it's important to note".
