# The Nebius Voice (product marketing house style)

Distilled from the Nebius PMM team's own copy, led by Kostas: the September 2026 customer messaging deck, the nebius.com homepage and the AI Cloud one-pager. Every rewrite and every line of report prose follows this voice. The examples below are verbatim from those sources.

## What the voice sounds like

Confident, warm and fluent. It speaks to the reader's work, not about our machinery. Lines have rhythm: pairs, contrasts and "from X to Y" arcs. Proof is shown, not crammed in.

## How a value prop or pillar is built

1. **A short title of two to five words**, often a pair or a promise.
   - "Build faster" · "Scale with confidence" · "Own your intelligence"
   - "Raw power. No surprises" · "Any user. Any workload" · "Elastic at any stage"
   - "Start quickly, scale flexibly" · "Build on your terms" · "Less complexity, better TCO"
   - "Predictable performance" · "Reliability across an owned stack" · "Faster access to the frontier"
2. **One flowing sentence** that says what the reader gets, usually 10 to 20 words. It reads aloud easily and never needs a colon to hold it together.
   - "Custom-built servers, racks and cooling enable end-to-end visibility and faster remediation without handoffs."
   - "Continuous stack improvements and acceptance tests deliver peak performance for scaling."
   - "Everything out of the box so you spend more time on what really matters."
   - "Access and serve the latest state-of-the-art open models as they launch."
   - "From small experiments to global-scale environments with flexible consumption options."
3. **The proof sits beside the copy, not inside it**, as a stat tile: a big figure and a short label.
   - "20x" with "FASTER TRAINING, FROM MONTHS TO SIX WEEKS"
   - "2x" with "THE EXPERIMENTS, $1M+/ A YEAR SAVED"
   - "80%" with "OF CUSTOMER TICKETS AUTO-RESOLVED WITH KIMI K2.5"
   - "99.9%" with "Dedicated endpoint SLA"

## Headlines

- Big, simple promises with an arc: "The AI-native cloud for production AI." "Built at the frontier. Optimized from power to outcomes." "From training to inference. On your terms."
- A position first; a rating or award supports it from a tile, a badge or the subhead.

## Customer-story lines

Lead with the transformation, often with a light turn of phrase:
- "Turns training speed and stability into film velocity"
- "Keeps researchers on robots, not cluster maintenance"

## Patterns to use

- **Pairs and triads:** "Any user. Any workload." "Security, observability, access management."
- **"From X to Y" arcs:** "from power to outcomes", "from months to six weeks", "from small experiments to global-scale environments".
- **The reader's payoff:** "so you spend more time on what really matters", "to fit your pace and budget", "so you can focus on what makes you different".
- **Plain, confident verbs:** build, scale, serve, move, run, deliver.

## Patterns that read as machine-written (never do these)

- **The colon formula:** "Raw power, no surprises: failed nodes reboot..." or "Fewer failed runs, lower spend: Rhoda saved..." Use a title plus a sentence instead.
- **Proof wedged into the sentence:** "..., rated Platinum in SemiAnalysis ClusterMAX 3.0." Put it in the tile or badge.
- **Parenthetical dates and citations** in copy: "(September 2026)", "(as of May 2026)". They belong in the evidence.
- **Mechanism before benefit:** "Nebius pulls the container image, configures the GPU and exposes your API endpoint." Say what the reader gets first.
- **Spec lists:** "with autoscaling, observability, function calling, structured outputs and self-service fine-tuning." Pick the one that matters.
- **Stacked clauses** joined by "and" three times. One idea per sentence.

Time to a running workload, not a booted cluster: Higgsfield started its first training run in under an hour." | **Faster time to AI value.** Go from sign-up to a running training job without waiting on infrastructure. *Tile: "<1 hour" / TO HIGGSFIELD'S FIRST TRAINING RUN* |

## Report prose

The report's own sentences (verdicts, notes, explanations) use the same voice: complete, flowing sentences in a confident, warm register. No clipped fragments, no colon chains, no telegraphese. Short is good; robotic is not.
