# Scoring Rubric

Every persona scores the copy on five criteria, 1 to 7. The anchors below are what each number means. Scores without a reason are not accepted.

## Fits their job (`jtbd`)
Does the copy speak to the job this person is trying to get done this quarter?
- **1:** Speaks to someone else's job. I can't see myself in it.
- **3:** Touches my world, but talks about the vendor, not my work.
- **5:** Names my job and a real problem in it.
- **7:** Names my exact job, the moment it hurts, and what changes for me.

## Believable (`credible`)
Would this person believe it, and could they check it?
- **1:** Reads as marketing. Claims I'd assume are false or unprovable.
- **3:** Plausible, but vague. No baseline, method or source.
- **5:** Specific and checkable, with some proof.
- **7:** Specific, dated, sourced, and I could verify it or call a reference.

## Meets the feeling (`emotional`)
Does it address what this person fears, or wants to feel (safe, in control, ahead, not embarrassed)?
- **1:** Ignores or triggers my fear (lock-in, a failed launch, a bad board meeting).
- **3:** Neutral. Informational only.
- **5:** Speaks to the fear or the ambition I actually carry.
- **7:** I'd feel safer, or more confident, putting my name on this choice.

## Stands out (`different`)
Is it different from what competitors say?
- **1:** Every vendor says this. Swap the logo and nothing changes.
- **3:** A familiar claim with a slightly different angle.
- **5:** A claim few competitors make, or a familiar claim with proof they lack.
- **7:** Something only this vendor could credibly say, and it matters to me.

## Clear (`clear`)
Can this person tell, in one read, what is offered, to whom, and what to do next?
- **1:** I can't tell what the product is.
- **3:** I get the category, but not what I'd buy or do.
- **5:** Clear offer and audience. Next step is findable.
- **7:** Instantly clear. I know what it is, if it's for me, and what to do.

## Claim verdicts
For each numbered claim: **lands** (would move me), **weak** (true but doesn't move me), **fails** (I'd push back or stop reading).

## Veto (buyers only)
A buyer vetoes a claim when it fails on believability in a way that would stop the deal: "Is that in the contract?" with no answer, a number with no baseline, a promise that shifts risk to the buyer.
